Rules of Gebeta (2×6 Educational Form)

Clear Ethiopian educational variant used for teaching counting and strategy.

Setup

  • 2 rows of 6 pits (homes / ቤት). Each starts with 4 seeds (48 total).
  • Two end stores hold captured seeds. Player A = bottom row, Player B = top row.

How to Move

  • Choose one of your non-empty pits. Sow seeds one-by-one anti-clockwise.
  • Multi-lap: If the last seed lands in an occupied pit, pick up all seeds there and continue. Turn ends only when the last seed lands in an empty pit.

Capturing Fours

  • Whenever a pit reaches exactly 4 during sowing, those 4 are captured by the owner of the pit.
  • Exception: If the final seed of a sowing makes a four, the sowing player captures them.

End of Game

  • Pass when you have no seeds on your side. After three consecutive passes by one player the game ends.
  • Remaining seeds go into the respective stores. Highest captured total wins.

History & Cultural Significance

Gebeta (ገበጣ) is an Ethiopian and Eritrean form of the ancient African count-and-capture family of games. Archaeological evidence from Yeha, Axum and Matara shows boards and rock-cut pits from the 6th–7th centuries AD (Aksumite period).

A 14th-century Ge’ez text by Giyorgis of Segla mentions qarqis, referring to both Gebeta and Ethiopian chess (Senterej). The game has long been played by children and elders, often dug into the earth or carved in wood, and appears at social gatherings and after harvest.

Modern Ethiopian educators value Gebeta for culturally responsive mathematics: counting, directionality, prediction and strategic thinking while staying rooted in local heritage.
